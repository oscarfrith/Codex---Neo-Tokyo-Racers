# Exotic concept art: shared brief (batch 1: Wedge and Hyper)

Status: design exploration, 2026-10-02. Not approved, not game content.

Goal: concept art a 3D artist can model from in Blender. Each cockpit and module is drawn on its own, then the same parts are shown assembled in different combinations. A part must look the same in every image it appears in.

## Method

Every image is generated by Codex from a **blockout screenshot** taken in Roblox Studio (Space Racers Backup v2, `Workspace.VehicleCategoryBlockouts._ConceptBooth_EXOTIC`). The screenshot fixes the camera, the proportions, the layout and the colour zones. Codex only redesigns the surface.

Source screenshots: `output/vehicle-categories-2026-10-01/exotic-art/src/`.

Command (run from the repo root, in the foreground, with a 10 minute timeout):

```
py -3 scripts/vehicle_blockouts/gen_image.py <out.png> <prompt.txt> --jpg <small.jpg> --ref <reference 1> <reference 2> ...
```

The prompt must say what each reference is, in order ("Reference 1 is ...").

## View

One near-orthographic three-quarter view per part, the one in its screenshot: front three-quarter for front parts, rear three-quarter for rear parts and jets. Assembled vehicles get both. Never change the camera angle.

## Colour code (the game's paint channels)

The same in every image, so parts stay comparable and the artist can read the paint channels:

| Colour in the art | Paint channel | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Glossy red | Primary | Body paint |
| Satin light silver | Secondary | Accent panels, casings, wings |
| Matte graphite | Detail | Mechanical parts, vents, linkages, undersides |
| Dark tinted glass | Glass | Windows |
| Cyan-white glow | Neon and Thrust | Lamps, light strips, jet exhaust |

## Style block (use verbatim as the last part of every prompt)

> Style: finished concept art for a stylised low-poly hover-racing game. Chunky faceted hard-surface forms, crisp chamfered edges, inset panel lines and shut lines, small vents, bolt heads and thin glowing trim strips, clean flat materials with soft studio lighting. A low-poly game model a 3D artist could build, not a photoreal render. No dirt, no weathering, no decals.
> Colour code, keep it exactly where the blockout has it: glossy red is body paint; satin light silver is accent; matte graphite is mechanical detail; dark tinted glass is windows; cyan-white glow is lamps and jet thrust.
> Plain light-grey seamless studio background, soft shadow below, the subject floating. There are no wheels, tyres, rims, rings or discs anywhere: the vehicle flies on jets. No text, labels, logos, numbers, arrows, callouts or dimension lines.

## Part prompts

Structure, in this order:

1. One paragraph: what the part is, in plain words (name, slot, what it does, its main shapes). Say "ONE modular part of a hover-jet supercar". For a paired or four-off part say that one unit is shown.
2. "Reference 1 is a grey-box blockout screenshot of this exact part. Keep its exact camera angle, proportions and outline, and the number and position of its elements. Keep each colour zone where it is. Turn the crude blocks into a finished hard-surface design." Then name three or four details to add that suit this part. Say which faces must stay flat and plain because other parts bolt on there.
3. "Reference 2 shows the game's existing vehicle style. Match its level of detail and finish, not its shape or colours."
4. "Show only this one part. No car around it."
5. The style block.

References for a part: Reference 1 is its screenshot in `src/`; Reference 2 is `src/style_piercer_ingame.png`.

## Checks for every part image

Open the JPEG next to its blockout screenshot and compare.

1. Same camera angle and framing as the screenshot.
2. Same outline and proportions. Same number of main elements in the same places (count lamps, nozzles, fins, vents, uprights).
3. Colour zones match the code and sit where the blockout has them.
4. Only the one part is shown. No car body, no extra parts.
5. No wheels, tyres, rings or discs. A jet nozzle seen end-on is fine if the body behind it is longer than it is wide.
6. No text, labels, logos, numbers or arrows.
7. It looks like a finished game asset, not a grey box and not a photo.

Regenerate with a tightened prompt if a check fails. Two extra tries at most.

## Where things attach (for the artist notes)

- **Cockpit (cabin):** owns all glass and the roofline. Its front and rear faces are flat bulkheads where the nose and engine deck bolt on. Its sides are flat pads for the side pods. Two buttresses run back from the roof either side of the engine bay.
- **Nose (FrontBody):** bolts to the cabin's front bulkhead. Carries the scuttle under the windscreen, a blanked arch each side for a stabiliser, and a flat pad underneath for the splitter.
- **Engine Deck (RearBody):** bolts to the cabin's rear bulkhead. Carries the flat engine bay pad on top (main turbine), a rail along each shoulder (side engines), a blanked arch each side (stabilisers), a notch in the tail (afterburner), two wing pads at the tail corners, and a flat pad underneath (diffuser).
- **Side Pods:** one each side, on the flat cabin side between the arches.
- **Main Turbine (Engine1):** sits on the engine bay pad behind the cabin, open to the sky.
- **Side Engines (Engine2):** a mirrored pair, one on each rear shoulder rail, nozzle pointing back.
- **Stabilisers:** four units, one in each arch, jet pointing down and back.
- **Afterburner (Boost):** in the tail notch, on the tail wall.
- **Splitter (FrontBumper):** under the nose. **Diffuser (RearBumper):** under the tail. **Wing (RearSpoiler):** on the two wing pads.

## Combination prompts

A combination is a whole vehicle: one cabin plus ten modules. The definitions are in `combos.json`. Each gets a front and a rear three-quarter image.

References, in this order:

1. The blockout screenshot of the assembled vehicle in this view: `output/vehicle-categories-2026-10-01/exotic-art/src/<id>__front.png` or `__rear.png`. It fixes the camera and where every part sits.
2. The parts sheet: `output/vehicle-categories-2026-10-01/exotic-art/sheets/<id>.png`. Eleven captioned concept images of the exact parts this vehicle is built from. It fixes what every part looks like.
3. Rear view only: the finished front image of the same vehicle, `output/vehicle-categories-2026-10-01/exotic-art/combos/<id>__front.png`.

Prompt structure:

1. One paragraph: "A complete hover-jet supercar assembled from modular parts, [front or rear] three-quarter view", and what kind of car it is.
2. "Reference 1 is a grey-box blockout screenshot of this exact assembled vehicle. Keep its exact camera angle and proportions and keep every part where the blockout has it."
3. "Reference 2 is a parts sheet: eleven captioned concept images of the exact parts this vehicle is built from. Draw every part with the design shown on the sheet: the same shapes, the same vents, the same number of lamps, nozzles, fins and strakes, the same colours. Do not invent new parts, do not leave any part out, do not restyle a part. Do not copy the captions or any text."
4. A part-by-part list, one line each: the part's name, where it sits on the vehicle, and its two or three identifying features (take them from `output/vehicle-categories-2026-10-01/exotic-art/parts_result.json`). Say how many units there are: four stabilisers (one in each arch), two side engines, two side pods.
5. "The parts are separate modules bolted together: keep a thin dark shadow gap at every seam between parts."
6. Rear view only: "Reference 3 is the finished front view of this same vehicle. This image shows the same vehicle, unchanged, from the rear."
7. The style block.

Checks for a combination image, against the blockout and the parts sheet:

1. Same camera angle and proportions as the blockout.
2. All eleven parts are present and in the blockout's positions. Nothing extra.
3. Every part matches its design on the sheet: shape, element counts, colours. Check each part in turn.
4. No wheels, tyres, rings or discs. No text, and no captions copied from the sheet.
5. Seams between modules are visible as thin shadow gaps.
6. It reads as one coherent, good-looking vehicle.
7. Rear view: it is the same vehicle as the front view.
